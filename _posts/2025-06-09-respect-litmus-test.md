Here's a thought experiment that will tell you everything you need to know about how your direct reports view your leadership:

Imagine someone offered you $500 for a 30-minute weekly meeting. Would you say, "That sounds nice, but can we meet every other week instead?"

Unless you're independently wealthy or doubt the person's ability to pay, the answer is obviously no. You'd protect that meeting time like a developer protects their uninterrupted coding blocks.

## The Leadership Currency

As a technical leader, your time and guidance are valuable currency. If your direct reports truly believe you can help them grow their careers, they'll treat 1:1 meetings with you like $500 weekly deposits into their professional development account.

### Signs Your Stock Is High

Your direct reports value your leadership when they:

1. **Protect 1:1 Time**
   - Show up prepared
   - Rarely cancel or reschedule
   - Actively engage during meetings

2. **Maintain Regular Communication**
   - Proactive Slack updates
   - Quick response times
   - Regular status sharing

3. **Seek Your Input**
   - Ask for career advice
   - Share challenges early
   - Request feedback on decisions

4. **Keep You Informed**
   - Share wins and losses
   - Provide context on team dynamics
   - Update you on project status

## What to Do If They're Not Buying Your Stock

If your direct reports aren't treating your time like it's valuable, here's how to turn things around:

### 1. Understand the Why
- Do they have a stronger relationship with another leader?
- Are they getting guidance from peers?
- Have you demonstrated value in past interactions?

### 2. Identify Their Needs
- Career growth objectives
- Technical development goals
- Current challenges
- Future aspirations

### 3. Deliver Real Value
- Provide actionable feedback
- Share relevant opportunities
- Make meaningful introductions
- Remove obstacles

### 4. Build Trust Through Consistency
- Honor meeting commitments
- Follow through on promises
- Maintain confidentiality
- Show genuine interest

## Creating Investment-Worthy Meetings

To make your 1:1s worth their time:

1. **Prepare Thoroughly**
   - Review previous notes
   - Track action items
   - Plan discussion points
   - Research their challenges

2. **Focus on Their Growth**
   - Discuss career trajectory
   - Identify learning opportunities
   - Share industry insights
   - Connect them with resources

3. **Make it Personal**
   - Remember their goals
   - Acknowledge achievements
   - Show genuine interest
   - Offer specific support

## Measuring Success

You'll know you're succeeding when:

- Direct reports come prepared
- They schedule additional time
- They refer others to you
- They implement your advice

## The Long Game

Building this kind of trusted relationship takes time. Think of it as compound interest - small, consistent deposits of value eventually create significant returns.

## Action Items for Leaders

1. **Audit Your Current 1:1s**
   - Meeting frequency
   - Engagement levels
   - Value delivered
   - Follow-through rate

2. **Evaluate Your Impact**
   - Career advancement of reports
   - Skill development progress
   - Problem resolution success
   - Team member retention

3. **Adjust Your Approach**
   - Tailor your style
   - Update meeting structure
   - Enhance preparation
   - Increase value delivery

## Conclusion

The true measure of your leadership isn't your title or your authority - it's whether your direct reports view time with you as an investment in their future.

If they're not treating your 1:1s like $500 meetings, it's time to increase your leadership market value.

Remember: Nobody turns down free money unless they don't believe it's real. Make sure your leadership value is both real and readily apparent.
